# Gulshan Polyols (NSE: GULPOLY)

> Research memo, not investment advice. Figures are from secondary aggregators and company releases found by web search in Nov 2026; anything marked *unverified* has not been checked against filings.

**Composite score 8.16/10** (rank 2 of the finalists) · market cap Rs 1,022 Cr · Chemicals

## 1. Two-minute drill

Gulshan makes grain-based ethanol (810 KLPD of distillery capacity) that it sells to the oil-marketing companies under India's blending programme, plus sorbitol, starch and calcium carbonate. Earnings jumped because the new distilleries ramped up: FY26 profit rose 332% to Rs 107 Cr. For this to keep working, OMC allocations must stay high, the procurement price must keep covering grain (FCI rice, maize) costs, and the oversupplied sorbitol market has to recover.

## 3. Thinking ladder

1. Why is a sorbitol maker suddenly earning 10x? Because the blending mandate created an assured buyer for ethanol at an administered price.
2. 2nd order: profit is now a spread between a policy-set price and a commodity cost, so margins swing on FCI rice and maize prices rather than on anything Gulshan controls.
3. 3rd order: once E20 is met nationally, allocation growth slows while the industry has overbuilt distillery capacity. Allocation cuts are the main risk; E27/E30 or sustainable aviation fuel would be the upside.

## 4. Customer and product angle

B2B and government. Ethanol goes to IOC/BPCL/HPCL (very high customer concentration); sorbitol goes to oral-care and pharma makers. What to check: Gulshan's share of OMC tender allocations for ESY 2025-26 and 2026-27, the sorbitol price trend, and whether the sorbitol buyers (toothpaste makers) keep reordering. Nothing to buy or visit.

*Provisional product score (desk-based, product not tried): 5/10.*

## 5. PE/VC view

Capex-heavy commodity spread with a 28% ROCE at the top of the cycle. Headroom is capped by policy-set volumes, a strategic exit is unlikely, and a P/E of 6 already reflects the market's view that these earnings are cyclical.

*Headroom score: 4/10.*

## 6. Red flags

- Profit is policy-driven: ethanol allocations and procurement prices are set by government/OMCs.
- FY25 CFO (Rs 41.8 Cr) and FY26 CFO (Rs 207.5 Cr) are very uneven; the 3Y profit CAGR is flattered by a weak FY25 base.
- Domestic oversupply in sorbitol and starch is squeezing the legacy business.
