# Frontier Springs (NSE: FRONTSP)

> Research memo, not investment advice. Figures are from secondary aggregators and company releases found by web search in Nov 2026; anything marked *unverified* has not been checked against filings.

**Composite score 8.17/10** (rank 1 of the finalists) · market cap Rs 1,697 Cr · Metal Fabrication

## 1. Two-minute drill

Frontier makes coil, forged and air springs, mostly for Indian Railways rolling stock (coaches, wagons, Vande Bharat). FY26 revenue rose 39% and profit 77%, at a 26.8% EBITDA margin, and management guides to over Rs 500 Cr of revenue in FY27. For that to happen, railway coach and wagon production must stay high, the new air-spring products must win approvals, and the push into auto and construction equipment has to work.

## 3. Thinking ladder

1. Why does a spring maker earn a 27% EBITDA margin? Because RDSO-approved vendor status for railway springs is slow to get and keeps competition small.
2. 2nd order: prices are set in L1 tenders, so margins hold only while the number of approved vendors stays small. New approvals would compress them.
3. 3rd order: the Vande Bharat sleeper and LHB conversion programmes drive air-spring volumes for several years, but railway budgets are cyclical and order flow is lumpy.

## 4. Customer and product angle

B2B. Customers are Indian Railways production units (ICF, RCF, MCF) and wagon builders, so concentration on one government buyer is very high. What to check: the count of RDSO-approved spring vendors, Frontier's tender win rate, payment days from the production units, and the non-railway revenue share.

*Provisional product score (desk-based, product not tried): 6/10.*

## 5. PE/VC view

Moderate capex and a 38% ROCE. Headroom is good while rolling-stock modernisation lasts. The exit path is index inclusion as the market cap grows, or a strategic buyer among auto-suspension groups.

*Headroom score: 7/10.*

## 6. Red flags

- Promoter holding is only 51.8% (the screener feed showed 59.2%).
- CFO is not verified; one source reports FY26 net cash flow of -Rs 1.7 Cr.
- Dependence on a single customer (Indian Railways).

**How the competitors are doing.** Railway-component peers (Texmaco, Oriental Rail, Pitti) grew sales 10-25% a year at 7-14% operating margins. Suspension leaders are larger and steadier: Jamna Auto at ~23x and Gabriel India (+15% FY26 revenue) at ~76x.

**Why this company is positioned differently.** Frontier's 27% EBITDA margin is roughly double its rail peers'. It comes from RDSO-approved status in coil, forged and air springs, where approved vendors are few, and from early volume on Vande Bharat and LHB air springs. FY27 guidance (Rs 500 Cr revenue) implies ~55% growth.

**Valuation versus peers.** P/E as scored 26.4x (on FY26 profit 27.7x). Peer median P/E: 23.5x across 7 peers, so it trades at a 12% premium. Fundamentals-implied P/E (screen-wide model, R² 0.17): 16.0x, so it trades at a 65% premium to what its ROCE, growth, size, leverage and sector would predict. ROCE 38.4% vs peer median 16.5%; 3Y sales CAGR 43.7% vs 13.3%.

**Why the valuation doesn't match.** At ~26x TTM (28x FY26) it sits above rail-ancillary peers (~15-17x) but far below the suspension leaders. The premium over rail peers reflects its margin and growth; the discount to Jamna and Gabriel reflects dependence on one customer (Indian Railways), L1 tender pricing that can be competed away, a low promoter stake with little institutional ownership, and unverified CFO. The market is pricing it as a high-quality railway vendor, not an auto-suspension franchise.
