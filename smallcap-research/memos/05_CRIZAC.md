# Crizac (NSE: CRIZAC)

> Research memo, not investment advice. Figures are from secondary aggregators and company releases found by web search in Nov 2026; anything marked *unverified* has not been checked against filings.

**Composite score 7.86/10** (rank 5 of the finalists) · market cap Rs 2,653 Cr · Education & Training Services

## 1. Two-minute drill

Crizac runs a B2B platform that links student-recruitment agents with universities. About 97% of revenue comes from the UK, and it earns a commission per enrolled student. FY26 revenue rose 23% to Rs 1,042 Cr and profit 41% to Rs 219 Cr, with strong cash generation (CFO about Rs 144 Cr). But Q1 FY27 revenue fell 4%. For the business to keep growing, UK international-student flows must hold up and other destinations have to start contributing.

## 3. Thinking ladder

1. Why do universities pay Crizac? Because recruiting international students directly across dozens of countries is expensive, and an agent network does it cheaper.
2. 2nd order: UK policy (the 2024 dependant-visa ban, Graduate Route changes, a proposed international-fee levy) feeds straight into Crizac's volumes.
3. 3rd order: financially stressed UK universities need international students more, but may also squeeze commissions or build direct channels. Diversifying to Canada or Australia doesn't help, because both are tightening too.

## 4. Customer and product angle

Two-sided platform: agents and universities. What to check: commission rates per enrolment, agent churn, university concentration, and compliance with the UK Agent Quality Framework.

*Provisional product score (desk-based, product not tried): 5/10.*

## 5. PE/VC view

Asset-light and cash-generative (FCF about Rs 127 Cr). Headroom is limited by destination policy. A strategic exit to a global education group (IDP, Navitas type) is plausible.

*Headroom score: 4/10.*

## 6. Red flags

- About 97% of revenue from the UK: extreme concentration on a single country's policy.
- Growth stalling (Q1 FY27 revenue -4%).
- The IPO was entirely an offer for sale, so promoters sold 20% and the company raised nothing.

**How the competitors are doing.** The global listed comparable, IDP Education, has lost roughly two-thirds of its value as the UK, Canada and Australia tightened student visas, and its earnings have collapsed. There is no Indian listed peer; the rest of the industry (ApplyBoard, Adventus, many agent networks) is private.

**Why this company is positioned differently.** Crizac is a B2B aggregator. Thousands of sub-agents route applications through its platform to UK universities, and it earns commission per enrolment. FY26 revenue grew 23% and profit 41%, so it has been taking share even as volumes softened, because universities are consolidating onto fewer, compliant aggregators.

**Why the valuation doesn't match.** At ~12-13x earnings with ~60% FCF conversion it looks cheap for an asset-light platform. The market is pricing in an IDP-style policy shock: 97% of revenue is UK, Q1 FY27 revenue fell 4%, and promoters sold 20% in an all-OFS IPO. The discount is a rational policy-risk discount. It narrows only if destination diversification shows up in the numbers.
