## 1. Two-minute drill

Gandhi Special Tubes makes precision seamless and welded steel tubes and nuts for automotive, hydraulic and boiler customers, and runs a wind-power business on the side. Its operating margin excluding other income is about 41%. FY26 revenue rose 11% and profit 16.5% to Rs 68 Cr, and it is returning cash through a Rs 15 dividend and a ~Rs 78 Cr buyback. For the case to work, its pricing has to hold in a small niche.

## 3. Thinking ladder

1. How does a tube maker earn 40% margins? High-precision, small-volume tubes that large mills don't bother making.
2. 2nd order: a small addressable market caps growth, which is why the cash goes back to shareholders.
3. 3rd order: Chinese imports or customers making the tubes themselves are the threats to pricing; the buyback puts a floor under the stock.

## 4. Customer and product angle

B2B. Customers are automotive tier-1s, hydraulic and boiler makers. What to check: top-customer share, repeat orders, and the share of profit coming from other income (treasury) versus operations.

*Provisional product score (desk-based, product not tried): 6/10.*

## 5. PE/VC view

Low capex but also low headroom. The value here comes from capital return, not from a scaling exit.

*Headroom score: 3/10.*
