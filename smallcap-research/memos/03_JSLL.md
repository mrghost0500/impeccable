# Jeena Sikho Lifecare (NSE: JSLL)

> Research memo, not investment advice. Figures are from secondary aggregators and company releases found by web search in Nov 2026; anything marked *unverified* has not been checked against filings.

**Composite score 7.89/10** (rank 3 of the finalists) · market cap Rs 3,728 Cr · Medical Care Facilities

## 1. Two-minute drill

Jeena Sikho runs Ayurveda hospitals, clinics and Panchakarma centres under the HiiMS brand. FY26 revenue rose 71% to Rs 801 Cr and profit 178% to Rs 222 Cr, at a 44% EBITDA margin. The company reports zero debt, though the screener shows D/E of 0.33, likely lease liabilities. Growth comes from adding beds, shifting toward private and retail patients, and a 51% stake in a UAE Ayurveda firm. For this to last, private patients have to keep paying and margins have to survive the shift away from scheme or panel business.

## 3. Thinking ladder

1. Why does a hospital chain earn a 44% EBITDA margin? Ayurveda beds need little equipment capex and staff costs are low.
2. 2nd order: that makes the margin sensitive to pricing power and to how reimbursement and empanelment work. A mix shift can move margins a lot.
3. 3rd order: AYUSH advertising and treatment-claim rules (e.g. 'reversal' claims for chronic disease) are a real regulatory and reputational exposure as the brand scales; trained vaids are a scaling bottleneck.

## 4. Customer and product angle

Consumer-facing, so this is a genuine 'try the product' case. Visit a HiiMS hospital or clinic, price a Panchakarma package, check whether health insurance covers it, and read the Google and Practo reviews and complaints, especially about efficacy claims. Record repeat-visit rates if management discloses them.

*Provisional product score (desk-based, product not tried): 5/10.*

## 5. PE/VC view

Fairly asset-light with a 45% ROCE and a large wellness market. Healthcare is a sector private equity likes. At a ~Rs 3,700 Cr market cap it is close to small-cap index inclusion.

*Headroom score: 7/10.*

## 6. Red flags

- A 44% EBITDA margin is unusual for healthcare and needs explaining (pricing, mix, government reimbursement).
- Marketing built on treatment claims carries regulatory risk.
- FY25 CFO of ~Rs 85 Cr (vs ~Rs 80 Cr PAT) comes from a secondary summary and is unverified.

The discount is mostly a credibility discount. Verifying revenue mix and cash conversion would show whether it is too large.
