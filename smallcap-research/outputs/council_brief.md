# Council brief: 15 Indian small-cap finalists (research, not advice)

Scores are 0-10 composites from a six-lens screen (Lynch 30%, Mayer 20%, Buffett/Fisher 15%, Kirby/Mukherjea 15%, PE/VC 10%, Lynch-product 10%) minus red-flag deductions. Data: stockanalysis.com screener (TTM) plus web-search research; CFO is unknown for several names. Rs Cr = crore rupees.

## Frontier Springs (FRONTSP)  score 8.25
- Mcap Rs 1,697 Cr; P/E 26.4 (FY26-PAT basis 27.7); ROCE 38%; 3Y sales CAGR 44%; YoY sales 39.2%; D/E 0.02; promoter 51.8%; FII+DII 0.5%; CFO/PAT unknown
- FY26 revenue Rs 322 Cr, PAT Rs 61.3 Cr. FY28 PAT bear/base/bull 65/100/130 -> implied P/E 26.1/17.0/13.1
- What it does: Frontier makes coil, forged and air springs, mostly for Indian Railways rolling stock (coaches, wagons, Vande Bharat). FY26 revenue rose 39% and profit 77%, at a 26.8% EBITDA margin, and management guides to over Rs 500 Cr of revenue in FY27. For that to happen, railway coach and wagon production must stay high, the new air-spring products must win approvals, and the push into auto and construction equipment has to work.
- Key risks: Promoter holding is only 51.8% (the screener feed showed 59.2%). | CFO is not verified; one source reports FY26 net cash flow of -Rs 1.7 Cr. | Dependence on a single customer (Indian Railways).

## Gulshan Polyols (GULPOLY)  score 8.16
- Mcap Rs 1,022 Cr; P/E 6.0 (FY26-PAT basis 9.5); ROCE 28%; 3Y sales CAGR 26%; YoY sales 33.0%; D/E 0.29; promoter 66.8%; FII+DII 0.3%; CFO/PAT 1.22
- FY26 revenue Rs 2,312 Cr, PAT Rs 107.2 Cr. FY28 PAT bear/base/bull 90/170/230 -> implied P/E 11.4/6.0/4.4
- What it does: Gulshan makes grain-based ethanol (810 KLPD of distillery capacity) that it sells to the oil-marketing companies under India's blending programme, plus sorbitol, starch and calcium carbonate. Earnings jumped because the new distilleries ramped up: FY26 profit rose 332% to Rs 107 Cr. For this to keep working, OMC allocations must stay high, the procurement price must keep covering grain (FCI rice, maize) costs, and the oversupplied sorbitol market has to recover.
- Key risks: Profit is policy-driven: ethanol allocations and procurement prices are set by government/OMCs. | FY25 CFO (Rs 41.8 Cr) and FY26 CFO (Rs 207.5 Cr) are very uneven; the 3Y profit CAGR is flattered by a weak FY25 base. | Domestic oversupply in sorbitol and starch is squeezing the legacy business.

## SRM Contractors (SRM)  score 7.95
- Mcap Rs 988 Cr; P/E 7.7 (FY26-PAT basis 8.9); ROCE 32%; 3Y sales CAGR 65%; YoY sales 94.0%; D/E 0.32; promoter 72.6%; FII+DII 0.3%; CFO/PAT 0.72
- FY26 revenue Rs 1,026 Cr, PAT Rs 111.0 Cr. FY28 PAT bear/base/bull 110/190/250 -> implied P/E 9.0/5.2/4.0
- What it does: SRM is an EPC contractor for roads, tunnels and slope stabilisation, with roots in Jammu & Kashmir and now national work such as a Rs 483 Cr Nashik Ring Road package. FY26 revenue roughly doubled to about Rs 1,026 Cr and profit to Rs 111 Cr, and FY27 guidance is Rs 1,400-1,750 Cr. For that, it must execute the Rs 1,800-3,000 Cr order book on time and get paid by government agencies without a large working-capital build.
- Key risks: FY25 CFO was negative (about -Rs 47 Cr on a -Rs 116 Cr working-capital build); FY26 CFO recovered to +Rs 92 Cr (Finology). | Concentrated promoter holding (72.6%) and a thin free float. | Fixed-price EPC risk, especially in tunnels.

## Jeena Sikho Lifecare (JSLL)  score 7.88
- Mcap Rs 3,728 Cr; P/E 17.4 (FY26-PAT basis 16.8); ROCE 45%; 3Y sales CAGR 49%; YoY sales 71.0%; D/E 0.33; promoter 63.6%; FII+DII 6.5%; CFO/PAT unknown
- FY26 revenue Rs 801 Cr, PAT Rs 222.0 Cr. FY28 PAT bear/base/bull 180/300/400 -> implied P/E 20.7/12.4/9.3
- What it does: Jeena Sikho runs Ayurveda hospitals, clinics and Panchakarma centres under the HiiMS brand. FY26 revenue rose 71% to Rs 801 Cr and profit 178% to Rs 222 Cr, at a 44% EBITDA margin, and the company is debt-free. Growth comes from adding beds, shifting toward private and retail patients, and a 51% stake in a UAE Ayurveda firm. For this to last, private patients have to keep paying and margins have to survive the shift away from scheme or panel business.
- Key risks: A 44% EBITDA margin is unusual for healthcare and needs explaining (pricing, mix, government reimbursement). | Marketing built on treatment claims carries regulatory risk. | FY25 CFO of ~Rs 85 Cr (vs ~Rs 80 Cr PAT) comes from a secondary summary and is unverified.

## Crizac (CRIZAC)  score 7.86

- FY26 revenue Rs 1,042 Cr, PAT Rs 219.1 Cr. FY28 PAT bear/base/bull 160/240/300 -> implied P/E 16.6/11.1/8.8
- What it does: Crizac runs a B2B platform that links student-recruitment agents with universities. About 97% of revenue comes from the UK, and it earns a commission per enrolled student. FY26 revenue rose 23% to Rs 1,042 Cr and profit 41% to Rs 219 Cr, with strong cash generation (CFO about Rs 144 Cr). But Q1 FY27 revenue fell 4%. - Key risks: About 97% of revenue from the UK: extreme concentration on a single country's policy. | Growth stalling (Q1 FY27 revenue -4%). | The IPO was entirely an offer for sale, so promoters sold 20% and the company raised nothing.
